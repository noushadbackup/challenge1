**What I conclude:**
1. **The score column matters only through the cut, and through which row the NMS keeps.** Official event F1 is threshold-free. After the 4 s NMS, no two rows compete for one fall, so re-ranking the kept rows changes nothing: HN and XHPN equal REF exactly, Δ = 0 in every fold. A hit score that also re-orders the NMS (H, XH) swaps the kept fragment in a few clips, and that costs fold 0 (−0.004 to −0.007).
2. **A hit-model cut is a better FP filter than a detector-score cut.**
   - Candidate AUC for hit@0.3 on SR-CV held-out folds: 0.936 against 0.883 for the detector score. For hit@0.5: 0.849 against 0.796.
   - With hindsight, the best cut is worth +0.011 on the hit score but only +0.005 on the detector score.
   - The hit-score F1 curve is flat between tau 0.30 and 0.64 (0.927–0.933). The detector curve is peaked (0.906–0.927).
3. **The headroom is small, so the rule cannot confirm it.**
   - Even the hindsight-optimal cut is only +0.011 pooled, below the bootstrap CI width (about ±0.015).
   - Fold 3 (already 0.975) never changes, so a pass needs strict gains on all of folds 0, 1 and 2.
   - The gain is concentrated in fold 1 (+0.018 to +0.029).
   - The best variant, XHTPFN, came out of about 20 variants scored on the same 20 SR-CV streams, so its P = 0.886 is, if anything, optimistic.
4. **The threshold is not stable across folds when chosen by nested CV.**
   - From training OOF: tau = 0 (no cut) in 12 of 15 cells.
   - From SR-CV OOF: 0.34–0.60.
   - The per-fold hindsight optima of the hit score are 0.68 / 0.64 / 0.22 / 0.0.
   - Only the fixed F1*/2 value (0.46) is stable, by construction.

## 1. (a) Event hit model

`uwbfall/hitmodel.py` adds a model that predicts whether each candidate event will hit a ground-truth fall.

**Features** (35 per candidate), computed from what predict-time already has:
- Detector: peak and mean of the gated blend, fraction of blocks ≥ 0.5, TCN and HGB member max/mean, detector duration.
- Segment classifier: P(fall clip) mean and max.
- Junctions: distance to the previous and next junction peak, segment length, replay junction distances.
- Replay: whether it was used, number of agreeing/finite crops, replay-vs-detector tIoU, refined duration, start/end shifts, crop start/end spread, mean crop-vs-median tIoU, low-quality and review fractions, anchor-mode agreement (radar anchor strength), onset spread, peak position.
- Local context: number of candidates within 4 s, score rank and gap among them.

**Targets:** hit@0.1/0.3/0.5, i.e. tIoU of the *refined* row with an evaluable GT fall ≥ θ. One LightGBM per θ, each averaged over 3 seeds (deterministic, force_row_wise). The score is the mean of the three, i.e. the expected mean hit over the ranking θs.

**Free rows:** a row that overlaps an ignore zone (±2 s, the official rule) and hits nothing is excluded from training (`train_ok`). It is neither a TP nor an FP for the evaluator.

**Two training sources:**
- **Training OOF (H\*, in-fit like the operating point).**
  - Each target's own G7-gated training-OOF candidates: about 345–360 per bag, of which about 160 are free, leaving **only about 190 usable rows**.
  - Inner AUC on these rows is unstable: for some bags it is below the detector score's (e.g. fold 1, tcn_bag2: hit 0.73 against detector 0.78–0.81).
- **SR-CV OOF (X\*, as W5 specified).**
  - For target fold k, the training rows are the held-out test-part candidates of the other 3 SR-CV folds; for val, all 4 folds. P means the 3 bags are pooled: about 2,300–2,700 rows, about 1,800 usable.
  - The subjects (fall and ADL) of the 4 test parts are pairwise disjoint (checked: 8 subjects per part, 0 shared), so no fold-k row ever trains the model that scores fold k.
  - Caveat: the fold-j detectors that produced those rows were trained on fold-train sets that include fold-k subjects. This is second-order and does not put fold-k rows into the training set.

**Ranking quality** (candidate level, before NMS, pooled-bag SR-CV model on the held-out folds; `out/info_XPN.json`):

**The score column on its own changes nothing.** The official evaluator uses the score only to order greedy matching, and after the 4 s time NMS no two rows compete for one fall:
- HN and XHPN replace the score of the reference's kept rows with the hit score. They are **identical to REF in every fold and on val** (Δ = 0.0000).
- H and XH also let the hit score decide which row the NMS keeps. They lose −0.0038 / −0.0073 in fold 0, where a lower-detector-score fragment with a higher hit score wins and is mislocalised. Hence the …N variants: they keep the reference NMS order, so the hit score only acts through the cut.

## 2. (b) Operating threshold and operating point

**Threshold cut after NMS** (`hitmodel.choose_threshold`). It maximises pooled official ranking F1 over a 0–0.6 grid, with a 3-point smoothed curve and the first maximum, scored with nested hit scores:
- Training-OOF variant: subject-grouped 5-fold inner CV.
- SR-CV variant: leave-one-training-fold-out inner models.

So no score the threshold is tuned on comes from a model that saw that row.

**Stability across folds** (bag × target cells):

| variant | tau chosen per cell |
|---|---|
| HT / HTN (training OOF, nested) | 0.0 in 12 of 15 cells; otherwise 0.40–0.58. The training-OOF curve is flat or noisy with about 190 usable rows |
| XHT (per-bag SR-CV, nested) | 0.0–0.60 |
| XHTP / XHTPN (pooled-bag SR-CV, nested) | 0.34–0.60 (per bag: uwb_default 0.44–0.60, tcn_bag1 0.34–0.48, tcn_bag2 0.48–0.60) |
| XHT…F (fixed) | 0.46 everywhere: the F1*/2 rule for calibrated scores (Lipton et al. 2014), with F1* ≈ 0.92 |
| T0 / XT0 (detector score) | 0.0–0.58 |

**Held-out threshold curve** (diagnostic only, chosen with hindsight, never used for selection; `taucurve.py`, 3-bag mean):

| score cut on | F1 at tau = 0 | hindsight best (tau) | range over tau 0.30–0.64 | per-fold hindsight argmax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| detector score (REF rows) | 0.9221 | 0.9273 (0.56) | 0.906–0.927 | 0.58 / 0.56 / 0.0 / 0.0 |
| hit score (XHPN rows) | 0.9221 | 0.9331 (0.64) | **0.927–0.933** | 0.68 / 0.64 / 0.22 / 0.0 |

The hit score has the better and flatter plateau, which is what W5 wanted. But the whole achievable gain is about +0.011, and folds 2 and 3 prefer almost no cut.

**XHTPFN's cut**, summed over 3 bags × 5 targets (`ign.py`):
- It drops 21 FPs, 3 TPs @0.3, 1 row that hits @0.1 but not @0.3, and 21 free rows.
- SR-CV FP @0.3 goes 20.0 → 14.0, TP 238.7 → 237.7, FA/h 2.43 → 1.70.
- On val, FP goes 2 → 0/1/1 per bag with no TP lost (0.9502/0.9524/0.9524 → 0.9646/0.9596/0.9596).

**Operating point re-selected on SR-CV OOF** (`opgrid.py` + `opsel.py`):
- Setup: all 60 points of the decode grid, every candidate replayed, about 3,700 new replays. The point is chosen on the other folds' pooled F1, nested.
- XOPd, using the detector-level criterion of `decode.tune`: Δ +0.0003 (P 0.49).
- XOPf, using the final criterion after replay and NMS: Δ −0.0062 (P 0.09).
- XOPf over-fits: it gains on the training folds (e.g. 0.906 → 0.939) and loses on the held-out fold.
- The current choice (training OOF, detector level) is as good as anything SR-CV can select.

## 3. (c) Ignore zones

`ign.py` covers 3 bags × (4 SR-CV folds + val), using the official ±2 s rule:
- **574** final REF rows overlap an ignore zone and hit no evaluable fall. Removing all of them changes the official ranking F1 in **0 of 15** cells, so they are indeed free.
- NMS: 9 rows that would hit @0.3 are suppressed by the time NMS, and **none** of them by a free ignore-zone row. Nothing in the pipeline suppresses a prediction *because* it overlaps an ignore zone.
- The hit model never learns free rows as negatives (`train_ok`). The threshold cuts 4–32 free rows per variant (summed over cells), which is harmless because they score nothing either way.
- The I2 loss-masking question (flagged clips as zero-weight training targets) is a separate issue, reported in I2.

## 6. Limitations and open issues

- **Selection on one SR-CV draw.** About 20 W5 variants (T0/H/HT/X*/R/OP and the N/F/P options) were scored on the same 20 SR-CV streams that R2_EVAL, I1 and I2 also used.
  - XHTPFN is the best of these and the N, P and F options were each added after earlier results were seen. Its P = 0.886 is therefore optimistic.
  - The F1*/2 rule (0.46) is a theory value, not tuned, but it was introduced after the nested taus had been seen.
  - Confirming a single pre-registered W5 candidate would need a fresh SR-CV regeneration (new composition seeds, so new detector bags).
- **Fold-clause ceiling.** Fold 3 (0.975) is unchanged by every cut. A pass therefore needs strict gains in folds 0, 1 and 2. Fold 0's best is +0.0006 (XHTPFN) and the gain sits mainly in fold 1.
- **Training-OOF hit model is data-starved.** After free rows are excluded, only about 190 usable rows per (bag, target) remain, and the nested tau collapses to 0 in 12 of 15 cells. Any in-fit W5 should use SR-CV-style held-out candidates (what the X variants do), which ties the deployable model to the SR-CV detectors.
- **Headroom.** With hindsight, the best cut on held-out folds is worth only +0.011 (hit score) or +0.005 (detector score). The large remaining errors are recall and localisation: Chute04 has no candidate in 6.3 of 55 falls per bag (I2), plus mislocalised rows in unmatched fall clips (I1). Neither can be fixed by a score or a threshold.
- Val moves +0.0096 for every hit-score cut variant (FP 2 → 0/1/1 per bag, no TP lost). That is consistent with SR-CV but within val noise (±0.04, 67 falls).
